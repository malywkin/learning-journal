## Definition

Sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS) is the sudden and unexplained death of an apparently healthy baby aged up to 12 months old.

## What causes SIDS?

The exact cause of SIDS remains unknown. However, certain risk factors increase vulnerability, including exposure to cigarette smoke and co-sleeping on sofas or chairs. Premature babies (born before 37 weeks) and those with low birth weight (under 2.5kg) face elevated risk, as do multiples. SIDS is most common in the first 6 months but can occur up to 12 months of age, during any sleep period.

## How to reduce the risk of SIDS

### Do

- Place baby on their back in the feet-to-foot position for sleep
- Reposition baby onto their back if they roll (until they can roll both ways independently)
- Keep baby in a separate cot or Moses basket in your room for at least the first 6 months
- Use a firm, flat mattress
- Keep the cot clear of pillows, duvets, bumpers, blankets, and soft toys
- Breastfeed if possible
- Consider using a dummy to settle baby to sleep
- Ensure baby receives routine NHS vaccinations

### Don't

- Never sleep on a sofa or armchair with your baby
- Do not smoke during pregnancy or around baby after birth
- Avoid bed-sharing if baby was born prematurely or had low birth weight
- Do not share a bed if you or your partner smoke, drink alcohol, use recreational drugs, or take sedating medications
- Prevent overheating; maintain room temperature between 16–20°C
- Avoid prolonged car seat use outside of travel

## Temperature management

Monitor baby's temperature by feeling their chest or the back of their neck. Reduce layers if baby shows signs of overheating. Use lightweight blankets or sleeping bags. Remove hats and extra clothing upon entering indoors or vehicles, even if it wakes baby. Never place baby near hot water bottles, electric blankets, radiators, or direct sunlight.

## Safe co-sleeping guidelines

If bed-sharing occurs, ensure baby sleeps on a firm, flat mattress on their back with pillows and blankets kept away. Do not swaddle; use baby sleeping bags instead. Prevent other children, babies, or pets from sharing the bed and ensure baby cannot fall or become trapped.

Bed-sharing is unsafe if baby was born prematurely or had low birth weight, or if any bed-sharer smokes, has consumed alcohol, uses recreational drugs, or takes sleep-inducing medications.

## Safe babywearing

Follow the T.I.C.K.S. guidelines: Tight (snug against body), In view (face visible at all times), Close (kissable head distance), Keep (chin off chest), Support (upright back support). Never feed baby in a sling.
