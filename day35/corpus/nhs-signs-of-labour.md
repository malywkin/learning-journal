## Know the signs

Several indicators suggest labour is starting:

- Contractions or tightenings
- A "show", when the mucus plug from your cervix comes away
- Backache
- An urge to use the toilet, caused by your baby's head pressing on your bowel
- Waters breaking

## When to contact your midwife or maternity unit

Contact your midwife or maternity unit for guidance if:

- You think you're in labour
- You're experiencing regular contractions every 5 minutes or more frequently

Call 111 if unable to reach your midwife or maternity unit.

### Urgent advice: call your midwife or maternity unit urgently if:

- Your waters break
- You have vaginal bleeding
- Your baby is moving less than usual
- You're less than 37 weeks pregnant and think you might be in labour
- Any contractions last longer than 2 minutes
- You're having 6 or more contractions every 10 minutes

Do not wait until the next day – call immediately, even if it's the middle of the night. Call 111 if unable to contact your midwife or maternity unit.

### Immediate action required: call 999 for an ambulance if:

- You think your baby is coming now and have a strong urge to push

## Latent phase of labour

The start of labour is called the latent phase, when your cervix becomes soft and thin, starting to open for birth. This can take hours or sometimes days. You'll likely be advised to remain at home during this time.

## What contractions feel like

During a contraction, your womb tightens then relaxes. For some people, contractions may feel like extreme period pains.

You may have experienced Braxton Hicks contractions during pregnancy — these are usually painless and don't build up consistently. Real labour contractions become longer, stronger and more frequent as your labour progresses. During a contraction, your womb muscles tighten and the pain increases. If you put your hand on your abdomen, you'll feel it getting harder.

## Backache often comes on in labour

You may experience backache or a heavy, aching feeling.

## A "show" can signal the start of labour

During pregnancy, a mucus plug sits in your cervix. This mucus comes away just before labour starts, or when in early labour, and it may pass out of your vagina. This sticky, jelly-like pink mucus is called a show.

It may come away in 1 blob or in several pieces. It's pink because it contains a small amount of blood. If you're losing more blood, it may indicate something is wrong — phone your hospital or midwife immediately.

A show indicates the cervix is starting to open. Labour may quickly follow or may take a few days. Sometimes there is no show.

## What happens when my waters break

Your waters may break during labour or before labour starts. Your baby develops and grows inside a bag of fluid called the amniotic sac. When it's time for your baby to be born, the sac usually breaks and the amniotic fluid drains out through your vagina.

If your waters break naturally, you may feel a slow trickle or a sudden gush of water you cannot control. Amniotic fluid is clear and pale. Sometimes it's difficult to tell amniotic fluid from urine. When your waters break, the water may be a little bloodstained to begin with.

Tell your midwife immediately if:

- The waters are smelly or coloured
- You're losing blood

If your waters break before labour starts, call your midwife. Use a period pad (not a tampon) so your midwife can check the fluid's colour.

### If labour does not start after your waters break

It's usual to go into labour within 24 hours of the waters breaking. You'll be offered an induction if you do not because, without amniotic fluid, there's a small increased risk of infection for your baby.

Tell your midwife immediately if:

- Your baby moves less than usual
- There's any change in colour or smell of fluid from your vagina

You should take your temperature every 4 hours when awake and inform your midwife if it's raised (usually above 37.5°C).

There's no evidence that having a bath or shower after your waters have broken increases your risk of infection, but having sex might.

## How to cope when labour begins

At the beginning of labour, you can:

- Walk or move about, if you feel like it
- Drink fluids — sports drinks may help maintain energy levels
- Have a snack, if you feel like it
- Try relaxation and breathing exercises; your birth partner can help
- Have your birth partner rub your back for pain relief
- Take paracetamol according to packet instructions — paracetamol is safe to take in labour
- Have a warm bath
